Autopsy №001: Copying Congress's stock trades

SPECIMEN
Congressional trade mirroring ("the Pelosi tracker")
CLAIM
Members of Congress trade on privileged information; copying their disclosed stock purchases beats the market.
VERDICT
DEAD
CAUSE OF DEATH
No aggregate edge exists even with impossible same-day execution. The real 28–45 day disclosure lag makes a bad trade slower.
SAMPLE
4,215 purchase disclosures · 1,046 tickers · 2021–2026
DATE OF DEATH
July 2026

The pitch

You've seen the screenshots: a senator's suspiciously well-timed semiconductor purchase, a speaker's household beating every hedge fund on Earth. There are apps that will mirror congressional trades into your brokerage account automatically, newsletters that alert you the moment a disclosure drops, and a durable internet consensus that STOCK Act filings are a legal insider-trading feed — smart money, in public, for free.

The test

We took every congressional purchase disclosure from 2021 through mid-2026 — 4,215 filings across 1,046 tickers — and ran a standard event study on dividend-adjusted daily prices (99% of events had usable price data). Performance is measured as alpha versus the S&P 500, not raw return: a strategy that merely rides the index deserves no credit in a bull tape.

Two clocks matter, so we tested both:

  • Transaction-date anchor — buy the moment the member did. This is impossible in practice (you can't see the trade until it's filed), but it's the cleanest test of the core premise: is Congress smart money at all?
  • Disclosure-date anchor — buy when the filing becomes public. This is the trade the apps actually execute. In our sample the disclosure lag ran a median of 28 days, mean 40, with a tenth of filings arriving 42+ days late.

The result

Congress is not smart money — in aggregate it's mildly dumb money. At the transaction-date anchor, the impossible best case, mirrored purchases underperform the S&P 500:

Horizon (trading days)Alpha vs SPYt-stat
5−0.18%−2.31
10−0.28%−2.59
21−0.43%−2.77

Fewer than 47% of these trades beat the index at all. Read that table again: it's not "no edge," it's a statistically significant negative edge, at an execution speed no subscriber will ever have.

The actionable version — buying at disclosure — shows one seemingly positive cell: +0.28% over the first five days (t = 3.59). It does not survive contact with its own distribution. The hit rate is 49.9% — a coin flip — and the median event return is exactly 0.00%: the average is being dragged positive by a handful of right-tail outliers, not lifted by a repeatable edge. The effect decays to nothing by day 42, the t-statistic is inflated because disclosures cluster (many filings, same day, same ticker — these are not independent observations), and whatever residue remains barely clears trading costs — before you race the fleet of copy-trading bots executing the same filing within seconds of publication.

Two more nails:

  • All of the year-level edge lives in 2022 (+2.45%, t = 4.06). Every other year is flat. A strategy that worked once, in one regime, is a backtest artifact wearing a suit.
  • The big trades are the worst trades. Purchases over $250k — the ones the apps trumpet as highest-conviction — ran −2.02% versus the index over the following month. Position size carries no information.

Why people believe it anyway

Because the story is perfect and the counting is selective. A committee chair buying a defense stock before an appropriations bill is a screenshot; the four thousand mundane purchases that lagged the index are not. Nobody posts the full distribution, so the audience only ever sees the right tail. This is survivorship bias with a congressional seal on it.

What would resurrect it

The aggregate is dead, but our test doesn't rule out concentration in a few members or committee-timed subsets. Fair warning: with 500-odd filers, member cherry-picking is a p-hacking machine — any honest revival needs a pre-registered member subset chosen on prior information, then validated on out-of-sample filings. If someone runs that properly and it survives, we'll publish the resurrection with the same prominence as the burial.
