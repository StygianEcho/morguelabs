Autopsy №005: The congressional-trading ETF that beats the market

SPECIMEN
The argument that a congressional-disclosure ETF's outperformance proves Congress has a tradeable edge
CLAIM
NANC tracks what Democratic members of Congress disclose buying, and it has beaten the S&P 500 since launch. Therefore the information edge is real and you can buy it.
VERDICT
DEAD
CAUSE OF DEATH
The margin over the index is beta, not selection. Risk-adjusted alpha is -0.9%/yr at t = -0.37. A plain Nasdaq-100 fund beat it by 19 points at a third of the fee, and the sibling fund tracking the other party underperformed by 15 points and is no longer active.
SAMPLE
40 monthly total returns · 2023-03-31 → 2026-08-11 · NANC, KRUZ, SPY, QQQ, dividend-adjusted
DATE OF DEATH
August 2026
Where this came from. A reader replied to Autopsy №001 with a Morningstar page showing NANC ahead of the index and a fair question: what am I missing? They were not missing anything. The fund really has outperformed. This is the answer to what that outperformance is made of.

Unlike our other case files this is not a pre-registered test of our own hypothesis. It is a descriptive analysis of public prices, run after the question was asked. That matters for how much weight it carries, so the method is stated in full and both plausible benchmarks are reported rather than the one that flatters the conclusion.

The pitch

Members of Congress vote on the industries they invest in, and they file their trades. Two ETFs launched in February 2023 to package the idea: NANC holds what sitting Democratic members and their families disclose buying, and KRUZ did the same for Republicans. The pitch writes itself, and the receipts look excellent. Since launch NANC has returned +108.8% against the S&P 500's +96.1%. Nearly thirteen points of outperformance, in public, in a real fund, with real money.

That is a stronger piece of evidence than any backtest, including ours. So we took it seriously.

The test

Total returns from dividend-adjusted monthly closes, 2023-03-31 through 2026-08-11, for NANC, SPY and QQQ. Forty monthly return observations. We regress NANC's monthly returns on each benchmark to split the result into the part explained by market exposure (beta) and the part that is not (alpha). No risk-free adjustment, which if anything flatters NANC slightly.

The only judgment call is which benchmark. We report both: SPY, because that is the comparison being made, and QQQ, because NANC's holdings look far more like the Nasdaq-100 than the S&P 500.

The first thing to notice is that NANC is more volatile than the index it beat. It is not a market-neutral stock-picking sleeve, it is a portfolio carrying more risk. The regression prices that exactly:

Beta 1.14 is the whole story. A portfolio holding 14% more market risk than the S&P 500, in a period when the S&P returned about 22% a year, should return roughly 25% a year while displaying no skill whatsoever. NANC returned 24.5%. What is left over after paying for the risk is negative nine-tenths of a percent a year, and statistically indistinguishable from nothing.

The holdings say the same thing in plainer language. NANC is 39.3% information technology and 13.0% communication services, with NVIDIA alone at 10.45% of the fund, then Microsoft 7.5%, Alphabet 5.7%, Amazon 5.3%, Apple 4.2%. Energy is 0.00%. That is a mega-cap technology tilt held through the exact window in which a mega-cap technology tilt was the only thing that mattered.

And it was not even the best way to own that tilt. QQQ, a fund that requires no disclosure filings, no thesis about Congress and no active management, returned 19.3 points more over the identical window while charging 0.20% instead of 0.72%.

The fund nobody links to

Unusual Whales launched two of these. NANC tracks Democrats. KRUZ tracked Republicans, on the same premise, with the same structure, at the same time.

Over its life KRUZ returned +33.2% against the S&P 500's +48.7%, underperforming by fifteen points. Its price history stops on 2025-03-20 at $55.9M in assets, and ETF Database now lists it as no longer active.

This is the part that should end the argument. If congressional disclosure carried usable information, it should not have been party-specific. Two funds ran the same experiment. One tilted into the winning sector and beat the index without generating alpha. The other did not, lagged badly, and disappeared. The survivor is now offered as proof that the strategy works.

That is survivorship bias operating in the open, on real products, rather than hidden inside somebody's backtest. It is the same mechanism Autopsy №001 found inside the trade data: a handful of winners carrying a story that the median case does not support.

Why people believe it anyway

Because the headline number is true, and because the places you go to check it will confirm it. Fund screeners report trailing total returns, which is what those columns are for. None of them will tell you that a 1.14-beta fund beating a 1.00-beta index by two points a year is the expected outcome of taking more risk rather than evidence of insight.

The narrative also does a lot of work. "Politicians trade on what they know" is a satisfying story with a villain, and it survives contact with a chart showing the line going up faster. Checking whether the line went up faster per unit of risk, and whether the fund built on the identical premise for the other party is still trading, requires wanting the answer more than wanting the story.

What we are not claiming

The alpha estimate is −0.075% a month with a standard error near 0.20%, so the 95% interval runs from roughly −5.5% to +3.9% a year. Forty months cannot resolve that range. We can say NANC's margin over the S&P 500 is fully accounted for by market exposure. We cannot say the fund has negative skill, and anyone citing this as proof that congressional trading is worthless is overreaching in the opposite direction.

NANC is also actively managed rather than a mechanical mirror of filings, so it is not a clean test of the copy-trade in Autopsy №001. And our series begins 2023-03-31, missing the first seven weeks after the 2023-02-07 inception.

Nothing here is a criticism of the fund, which does exactly what it says it does and has delivered a perfectly respectable return. The specimen on the slab is the inference, not the product.

What would resurrect it

Positive alpha against a benchmark matched to the fund's actual risk and sector exposure, sustained long enough for the t-statistic to mean something, and present in congressional-disclosure portfolios of both parties. A raw return above the S&P 500 is not evidence. It is the thing that needs explaining, and beta explains it.
